- **‘oi’** appears in a wide range of bureaucratic and mundane contexts (accounting, garrison, boundary markers). A plural particle is plausible, but the example “ewehi ranuf oi onef ranuf” shows ‘oi’ between two nouns, which is atypical for a simple plural marker that would normally attach to the noun itself.  
- **‘fuhod’** is found in both elite (temple treasury, royal archives) and low‑status contexts (road checkpoints, burial deposits). If it meant “official,” we would expect it only where officials are recorded; its presence on a tomb offering label and a burial chamber deposit is inconsistent.  
- **‘ui’** is used across locational and institutional settings, which fits a locative preposition, but the example “dafu onef ui windu” places ‘ui’ before a proper name rather than a place noun, suggesting it may function more like a case marker than a preposition.  
- **‘razed’** is labeled as a “ritual‑item” yet occurs in purely administrative venues such as taxation registries and road checkpoints, where a ritual object would be unlikely; the breadth of contexts weakens the ritual‑item gloss.
